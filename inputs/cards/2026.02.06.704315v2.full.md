# Unexpected changes in reproductive barriers between incipient species after experimental evolution in sympatry

**Authors:** Flore
**Year:** 2026
**DOI:** 10.1101/2026.02.06.704315

## Abstract

- Hybridization is generally considered a temporary phenomenon, but it is actually widespread and may 15 last for large time periods between species that stably coexist. Here, to test whether evolving with a 16 closely-related species modifies or maintains partial reproductive isolation, we performed experimental 17 evolution in artificial sympatry vs. allopatry with two closely-related colour forms of spider mites 18 ( Tetranychus urticae ) that exhibit an asymmetrical pattern of pre-mating isolation despite almost com19 plete post-zygotic isolation. We assessed whether evolutionary changes occurred in traits associated to 20 (i) pre-mating isolation, (ii) post-mating pre-zygotic and early post-zygotic isolation, and (iii) late post21 zygotic isolation. Our results revealed that reinforcement did not occur even under forced long-term 22 sympatric evolution. Instead, the strength of some reproductive barriers decreased ( e.g. , premating iso23 lation and fertilization failure), and some trait changes indicated convergence rather than divergence 24 between species ( e.g. , mating propensity, latency to copulation). In fact, both types of males showed the 25 same decreased preference for red-form females across generations in sympatry. In line with this, traits 26 underlying fertilization success evolved in the same direction and with similar amplitude in heterotypic 27 crosses and in their homotypic control, as the offspring sex ratio of green-form females decreased in 28 sympatry irrespective of the male they mated with. Finally, other changes in reproductive barriers re29 sulted from trait correlations ( e.g. , decreased zygote mortality but increased juvenile mortality). Hence, 30 despite very high costs of hybridization, responses occurring following evolution in sympatry were un31 related to selection directly associated to hybridization, but rather the by-product of other evolutionary 32 forces, with cascading consequences for reproductive barriers.
