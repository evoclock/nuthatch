# Interpreting GC content differences across populations at polymorphic sites

**Authors:** Sheel Chandra, Ziyue Gao
**Year:** 2026
**DOI:** 10.1101/2026.05.16.725686

## Abstract

- 2 Recent studies have reported consistent inter-population differences in GC content at - 3 polymorphic sites in multiple species, including humans. Specifically, populations that - 4 experienced recent bottlenecks exhibit lower average GC content (GC%) at common - 5 polymorphic sites compared to non-bottlenecked groups-an observation previously interpreted - 6 as indication of rapid evolution of base composition. In this study, we investigate the - 7 evolutionary and technical factors driving these patterns across humans, mice, maize, and - 8 silkworm. We find that GC% at polymorphic sites is highly sensitive to the allele frequency - 9 threshold applied. Relaxing this threshold reduces inter-population differences to negligible - 10 levels in humans and significantly attenuates similar signals in other species. We further - 11 observe substantial GC% variation across allele frequency bins, a pattern driven by the - 12 differential abundance of different mutation types. We demonstrate that these observations are - 13 collectively driven by an interaction between demographic history and a universal excess of - 14 strong-to-weak mutations relative to weak-to-strong mutations, which is counteracted by GC- - 15 biased gene conversion (gBGC) over long evolutionary timescales. Forward-in-time simulations - 16 with realistic parameters recapitulate observed patterns of GC% variation across both - 17 populations and allele frequency bins. Overall, our findings reveal that the base composition at - 18 polymorphic sites is strongly shaped by the interaction between demographic history, mutation - 19 bias, and gBGC, and does not represent stable, genome-wide trends. Consequently, inter- - 20 population differences in GC content-especially at common variants-should not be - 21 interpreted as evidence of ongoing divergence in base composition or shifts in mutation - 22 patterns.

- forward-in-time simulations
